06attaching the weather

A month of sales is 1,367 rows — but only 232 independent weather readings.

Weather varies by store and day, not by product category.

232
independent weather readings
8 stores × 29 selling days
→each one shared by every category in that store-day
1,367
rows in the sales table
store × day × category
So 1,367 rows is not 1,367 pieces of evidence about weather. Every claim that follows carries that caveat.

06bauditing the source

The weather archive quietly rewrote its own history.
We keep receipts now.

5 store-days changed after we locked our numbers. Two crossed our rain line.

↓
S007 08-07 2.3mm to 0.9mm (rain to dry); S008 08-07 0.4mm to 3.7mm (dry to rain) Both fall on the newest date in our window — exactly how a preliminary value behaves before it settles.
×
Our cache deleted the previous pull every single run The cache was named by date range, and the range grew daily — so each run destroyed the only record of what the archive had said before. Found in audit; pulls accumulate now.
That doesn't bring anything back. Our numbers reproduce because they're pinned to a dated snapshot — which is luck, not design.

07what weather actually did

Rain doesn't change how much people buy.
It changes how they pick it up.

3,721 orders, 2.76σ, p = 0.006 — but orders in the same store-day aren't independent, so: suggestive, not proven.

08the finding we killed

The +14% rain revenue boost fell apart under testing — so we don't claim it.

And the rainiest store is also the richest — S001: 86% rainy days, $6,673 median day against S006: 14% rainy days, $4,714 median day. Pooling lets that impersonate a rain effect.
